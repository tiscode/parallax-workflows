## Introduction

The Chicago Global identity is the firm's promise of institutional rigor and practical accessibility. These guidelines exist so the brand reads as one thing whether a reader encounters us in an investor letter, a Parallax dashboard, a partner-co-branded newsletter, or a slide deck. Consistency is what lets a small firm punch above its weight.

### Two principles

First, **the brand is one, but it has two surfaces**. Chicago Global, the firm. Parallax, the product. Same palette, same typography, same voice. The two surfaces share the system; what differs is how each surface emphasizes elements of it. The *"Intel Inside"* model — Parallax appears inside Chicago Global deliverables with a "Powered by Parallax" tag, never the other way around.

Second, **every claim earns its place**. Every analytical number is anchored. Every visual rule has a measurement. Every banned word has a replacement.

### Who this applies to

These guidelines apply to all employees of Chicago Global Capital Management, LLC and Chicago Global Pte Ltd, and to all external partners when they reference Chicago Global or Parallax in any communication. External use requires a partnership agreement and is subject to the rules in Part 5.

## 2. Brand architecture

Chicago Global is the firm. Parallax is the product. The relationship is structural and reflects the order in which a reader encounters us in the wild.

**Chicago Global** is the parent — the asset management entity, the regulated counterparty (CGCM LLC in the US, Chicago Global Pte Ltd in Singapore under MAS CMS101590), the signatory on investor letters and partnership agreements.

**Parallax** is the product Chicago Global built and now licenses — the AI-native portfolio intelligence engine. It has its own surface (a dedicated site, dashboards, API).

### Surface mode follows the medium, not the brand

Light versus dark is not a brand rule. It is a medium rule. The two brands share the system; each surface picks the mode that fits the medium.

| Context | Default mode | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Parallax console / dashboard | Dark | Data density, always-on screen |
| Parallax brochure or slide insert | Light | Print-adjacent, mixed audience |
| Chicago Global investor letter | Light | Long-form reading, printed or emailed |
| Chicago Global newsletter, special edition | Either | Design system explicitly allows dark |
| Chicago Global title or cover slide | Either | Navy-heavy backgrounds standard for title slides |

The rule: when in doubt, light. Dark mode is a deliberate choice for data-dense screens and for high-impact moments.

# Part 2 — Verbal Identity

## 3. Voice

Chicago Global writes as a **skeptical senior colleague**, not an assistant. We assume the reader is competent and busy. We are direct, push back when something is wrong, say "I don't know" rather than fabricate confidence, and skip preambles, padding, and sycophancy. Dry wit welcome.

Every analytical claim is anchored to a number — never "markets declined sharply" but "markets fell 10%." The institutional first-person plural ("we," "our," "the Fund") is the standard voice.

### The five-writer composite

- **Michael Lewis** — clarity for non-specialists
- **John McPhee** — structural discipline, technical detail
- **Christopher Hitchens** — word-level precision
- **Joan Didion** — rhythm, telling detail
- **Warren Buffett** — conversational authority, dry self-deprecation

### Point of view

Institutional "we / our" — the voice of the firm, not one person. Even when an investor letter is signed by Ben or Ivan, the body uses "we." The exception is the Marco Voss persona (see §6).

### By audience

- **Platform (digital banks, digital brokers).** Technical-collaborative. API-and-MCP-first framing. Emphasize integration depth, latency, audit trail.
- **Institutional (banks, asset managers, large corporates).** Technical-precise. Methodology-first. Reproducibility statements explicit.
- **Advisory (RMs, wealth managers).** Collaborative. Address the RM's three recurring questions: correction vs. regime, rotate or not, can I share this with clients.

For cross-cultural emphasis modifiers, see §8.

## 4. Hard rules

Non-negotiable.

1. Every analytical claim anchors to a number
2. No hedge phrases ("it's worth noting," "it bears mentioning")
3. No "not just X, but Y" constructions
4. Maximum two em-dashes per piece
5. No bullet points in prose unless explicitly requested
6. Active voice by default
7. No false agency. Name the human actor
8. No Wh-/How throat-clearing openers
9. No exclamation marks. Ever
10. Vary sentence rhythm

### Connective tissue

| Function | Use |
|---|---|
| Contrast | but, however, yet, while, despite |
| Consequence | hence, as a result, this should |
| Surprise | in fact, still, even as |
| Accumulation | moreover, furthermore, in addition |

**Avoid:** "that said," "on the other hand," "it should be noted that."

### Quality checks

1. Earn every sentence
2. Include one concrete example and one explicit tradeoff
3. State one thing you are *not* claiming
4. If a sentence sounds like marketing, rewrite it as a memo
5. Verify every number

## 5. Banned words

| Banned | Replace with |
|---|---|
| delve | examine, look at |
| tapestry | mix, structure |
| nuanced | (describe the nuance) |
| multifaceted | (enumerate the facets) |
| navigate | handle, work through |
| robust *(marketing)* | reliable, holds up |
| leverage *(verb)* | use |
| holistic | full, complete |
| comprehensive | full |
| seamless | (skip) |
| cutting-edge | (skip — name what it does) |
| revolutionary | (skip) |
| game-changing | (skip) |
| world-class | (skip — show, don't claim) |
| underscore *(verb)* | show, demonstrate |
| crucial | important, central |
| vital | important |
| realm | area, field |
| myriad | many |
| embark | start, begin |
| unlock | open, enable |
| empower | give, allow |
| synergy | (describe the interaction) |
| landscape *(metaphorical)* | (describe the field) |

**Carve-out:** "robust" is fine in technical/statistical usage ("robust standard errors").

### Banned constructions

- "Not just X, but Y"
- Three-part lists ending in vague abstraction ("speed, accuracy, and excellence")
- Hedge phrases that buy time
- Wh-openers as throat-clearing
